## Proposal

Hi,

*That's No Moon* is compelling because its unsettling idea only works if the images remain believable. I understand the direction you are describing: not glossy “AI sci-fi,” but familiar documentary and archival language slowly giving way to something that feels wrong beneath the surface.

I’m an AI-native visual artist working across **Veo, Kling, Midjourney, Flux, and Google’s image tools**, with Photoshop used as the critical finishing layer. My workflow is built around locking the visual rules before generating at scale: film-stock and lens references, lighting direction, material behavior, camera distance/movement, and a restrained color pipeline. I then use Photoshop for compositing, grain matching, cleanup, and grade consistency so the work feels optical rather than generically generated.

The two portfolio pieces I would point you to are my **LUMINA Eclipse Diver on Obsidian Glass** hero shot and **Urban Night** wrist shot. They are not sci-fi scenes, but they show the parts of the craft most relevant here: controlled low-key lighting, physically convincing reflections, understated atmosphere, and realism that does not announce itself as AI.

For your film, I would begin each sequence with a compact visual bible and a small set of approved anchor frames. From there, I would build image plates and video variants in Veo/Kling, then match grade and texture in post so Apollo-era archive, sterile control rooms, and the buried lunar architecture live in one coherent world. The reveal language would stay quiet: dust, scale, acoustics, aged metal, and barely perceptible geometry—not spectacle.

For the $1,500 trial month, I suggest agreeing on one clearly defined opening sequence or shot package first. That gives us room to establish the visual grammar, feedback cadence, and approval standard before we scale to the wider monthly footage target.

I’m available for an ongoing collaboration and comfortable discussing the creative decisions directly as we build the film. I’d be glad to talk through the first sequence and the visual references you want to protect.
