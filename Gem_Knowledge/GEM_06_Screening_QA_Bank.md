## Q: "How do you prevent AI images/videos from looking like AI?"

> Lighting consistency is the single most critical factor in realism — more so than resolution or detail level. If the light direction, temperature, or softness on the product differs by even 5 degrees from the background environment, the human brain instantly registers it as fake, regardless of how sharp the image is.
>
> My process: I use Google Nano Banana 2 to generate the base, then move into Photoshop to manually apply contact shadows, ambient occlusion, and grain matching between the product and the environment. I also apply micro-contrast adjustments that mimic real-world lens and sensor characteristics. The result is an image where the AI was purely the starting canvas, not the final product.

## Q: "What is an uncommon piece of knowledge you've learned about AI image generation?"

> Lighting consistency dictates realism far more than pixel resolution. If the direction, temperature, or softness of the light source on the product differs by even 5 degrees from the background environment, the human brain instantly registers the image as fake — no matter how sharp or detailed it is. This is why most "AI images" are immediately recognizable: the generation model gets the content right but gets the physics of light wrong.
>
> The fix is manual: match light direction, add real contact shadows, and apply ambient occlusion in Photoshop after generation. This single step accounts for 80% of why my output passes as real photography.
